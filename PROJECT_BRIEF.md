# Career GPS: project brief

## Goal
No child should give up a dream because the road looks invisible, confusing or unaffordable. Career GPS shows a child and their family, in their own language:
- the real road to a chosen career;
- what it may cost;
- the help that may be available;
- what to do this month.

## Who it is for
- **First:** parents and children in India with little schooling, especially lower-middle-class and rural families. The pilot state is Maharashtra (English and Marathi).
- **Later:** Canada and the US (a separate commercial version). Shown only as "Planned" until validated.
- **How it's used:** child and parent explore together. Free for families who cannot pay; revenue later from institutions (governments, CSR, schools), in a way that does not hurt families.

## What it does (current scope)
One complete, convincing journey for a fictional Maharashtra student exploring cardiology. No more careers until this works end to end.

Journey: Dream → Career reality → Routes → Where I am → Route Readiness → Cost scenarios → Support → Funding gap → Monthly estimate → Next three actions → Review date → Career Passport.

The building blocks:
- **Branching routes:** main, lower-cost, after an unsuccessful entrance attempt, and related careers. Each has decision points and shows the progress that carries over.
- **Route Readiness:** five areas (eligibility, preparation, affordability, opportunity, evidence confidence). Each is Ready / Gap / Unknown with a reason and one action. There is no overall score.
- **"Change something":** change budget, marks, location, entrance result or interest. The app says what changed, which routes are affected, and gives the new cost range and actions. Completed steps are kept.
- **Three owned next actions:** one each for the child, the parent and the school/teacher, each with a reason, a due date and a done state.
- **One reusable data model:** every career is a data pack on one schema. The musician journey is next and runs on the same screens.

## Rules that never change
1. At most 3 primary actions visible at once; pictures first; one big number per screen; details on tap; plain English and Marathi.
2. No sensitive question before value is shown. Budget, place and income are asked only where they change a route, cost or scheme, with a reason and Skip. Occupation and land are never asked.
3. Never invent a number. Every factual claim keeps its source, geography, effective date, retrieval date, review status and expiry rule. Unsourced items stay visible `[placeholders]`, labelled Example.
4. Nothing shows as Ready or Researched until a named person has checked the facts behind it.
5. Information, not financial advice. No product ranking, no "best/safe/guaranteed" wording, and a "talk to a registered adviser" button wherever savings appear.
6. Demo data only (fictional children). No accounts, analytics or real-child data until a privacy review is done.

## What "done" looks like for the current step
- A new user completes the cardiologist journey in under 12 minutes (still needs one timed walkthrough by a person).
- At least three genuinely different routes.
- Changing one assumption visibly changes the plan.
- No sensitive question before value is shown.
- The final screen gives three clear actions.
- Every screen works at 390px in English and Marathi.
- All claims are labelled Example until a person verifies them.
- The same schema runs a musician with no component change.

## Order after this
1. Musician journey on the same engine.
2. "Where to keep the money" explainer: government-backed savings, bank deposits and market-linked options; plain risk wording; no ranking.
3. Bank-by-bank comparison with dated official rates.
4. Study in India vs abroad.
5. Field check with real families, but only after a privacy review.

## Not in scope now
- Accounts or real-child data, a teacher dashboard, payments.
- A numerical feasibility score, six full careers, psychometric testing.
- Canada/US beyond "Planned" labels, and visual redesign.
