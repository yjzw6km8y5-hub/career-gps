## Final draft

### A. Mission and vision (shown in app and used as the build test)
- Mission: No child should give up a dream because the road looks invisible, confusing or unaffordable. In their own language, we show every family the real road, the real cost, the help available, and what to do this month.
- Vision: Every child grows up with a clear, honest, living plan for the future they choose, and the family knows how to support it.
- Tagline option: "Swapna se Sankalp tak" (from dream to resolve).
- Build test for every feature: would a parent with little schooling understand it and act on it this month? If not, simplify it or move it behind a tap.

### B. Example day in the life
- Per career, in India and abroad: a picture timeline of an example day, covering hours, where they work, stress, perks, and what they like and dislike.
- Each example names the role, place and career stage (e.g. "[role], [city], [years in career]").
- Sourced from practitioner interviews checked by a human reviewer. Where sources differ, show the range ("some days are shorter, some longer") rather than one day as typical.
- Video and audio later.

### C. Where to study and work: four tracks per career
1. Study in India, work in India. 2. Study in India, work abroad. 3. Study abroad, work abroad. 4. Study abroad, return to India.
- Show one track at a time: the family's chosen track, defaulting to track 1. The other tracks open on tap. Use pictures and audio.
- Each track shows: total cost (₹ and destination currency, with a dated exchange rate), years, extra exams or licences (e.g. for doctors abroad [licensing exams, to be sourced]), visa route, and pay range. Technical details open on tap.
- "How many make it" appears only where reliable data exists. Each figure states the outcome measured (admission, qualification, employment or sustainable income), the group counted, the country or state, the time period, the source and the date. Otherwise show "Reliable data not available."

### D. School choice by age
School types with fee ranges by state (government, aided, budget private, private CBSE/ICSE, international; Canada: public, Catholic, private). Fees are sourced and dated. The family picks a school per stage, and the choice feeds the money plan.

### E. Saving and investing (information only, not financial advice)
- Layered display, to keep to the 3-choice rule:
  1. First screen: at most 3 broad groups, with pictures and audio: "Government-backed savings", "Bank deposits", "Market-linked options". [Group names to be tested with low-literacy users.]
  2. On tap: the individual products in that group. India: SSY, PPF, NSC (government-backed); RD, FD (bank); mutual fund SIPs (equity, hybrid, debt), index funds, gold options [verify current schemes] (market-linked). Canada: RESP and other options [to be confirmed].
  3. Technical comparison table only behind "see details".
- Compare only factual attributes: lock-in period, minimum amount, tax treatment [verify], and whether returns are fixed or can change.
- The app does not rank or recommend products or allocations.
- Risk in plain words with icons, written per product from sourced facts. Examples, to be matched to each product by a human reviewer: "You may lose money", "The provider may fail to pay", "Rising prices can reduce what this buys", "Money is locked for [X] years", "You may not be able to take money out early [or only with a penalty]". Low day-to-day movement is never labelled "safe" or "low risk".
- Past returns are shown only on tap, as dated ranges labelled "Past results. Not a promise."
- Scenario view shows a range, never a single figure, with product-specific risk lines beside it. Labels describe what can happen ("Value can fall", "Value can rise and fall a lot", "Value is fixed by the provider's terms, but the provider may fail to pay"), not how safe a product is.
- Monthly amount: the main screen shows "About ₹[low]–₹[high] a month (estimate)", with "Estimate. Value may fall. Information, not financial advice." on the same screen. On tap it shows the inputs with dates, the assumptions, the risks, and how the range was calculated.
- Call to action: "For a personal plan, talk to a registered adviser" (SEBI-registered in India; licensed adviser in Canada).
- Any paid adviser link is disclosed and never affects the order or content of what the app shows.

### F. Compare 2–3 career tracks (the gamified core)
- Pick 2 or 3 careers to see one simple screen: a money-over-age line (cost years, then earning years), lifestyle, interest fit, and an example day.
- Barriers use three icon levels: "fewer barriers / some barriers / major barriers". The specific cost, eligibility, location and probability evidence is shown on tap. There is no numeric score.
- The child "chooses a track" like picking a game path and can switch at any time.

### G. Simple mode by default
Pictures first, a maximum of 3 choices per screen, voice and audio in the regional language, and one big number per screen. Details appear on tap. Tables are an optional "see details". This rule applies to the investing section (see E) as well.

### H. Interest assessment
- Use a licensed, validated tool, not a homemade one.
- It must be administered in the regional language, with audio, and tested for accessibility with low-literacy users.
- Results are always labelled "a guide to explore, not a decision about your child's future."

## Open points for the owner

- **ERROR** — “one simple screen: a money-over-age line … lifestyle, interest fit, and an example day” conflicts with the default rule of “one big number per screen” and may overload low-literacy users. Fix: split the comparison into short swipe/tap screens—money, daily life, interest fit, and barriers—with no more than three careers visible.

- **ERROR** — “own”, “official link”, or “gap with plan and date” treats an external link as competitor parity. A link may require another account, payment, higher literacy, or an unsupported language and therefore may not match the competitor experience. Fix: count an official link as parity only if the linked function is accessible to the target users at the required cost, language, literacy, and workflow; otherwise classify it as a gap.

- **UNCERTAIN** — “Starting checklist” may still omit competitor features, but no completed, dated competitor evidence is supplied. The proposed audit could resolve this, so the checklist must not be treated as complete before that audit. Fix: label it “non-exhaustive starting checklist” and require testing of complete user journeys, eligibility, pricing, languages, accessibility, and account requirements—not only feature names.

- **UNCERTAIN** — “Value may fall” is displayed beside every monthly estimate, including potentially fixed-term government or bank products. This could be inaccurate or obscure the actual risks of inflation, early withdrawal, deposit-insurance limits, or issuer failure. Product selection and jurisdiction-specific source evidence would settle it. Fix: show a short, product-specific risk statement on the main screen instead of the universal wording.
